Ibiza sunset spots: choose the setting before the journey

Want a promenade with places to sit, a horizon scattered with islets, or a northern cove framed by hills? Ses Variades, Platges de Comte and Benirràs are different plans. Choose one that fits your base and the return you can actually arrange.

Three ways to spend the last light

Sant Antoni · Promenade plan

Ses Variades

The tourism office describes a coastal pedestrian route north of Sant Antoni’s bay, continuing toward Es Caló des Moro. Benches and rest areas make the promenade a different proposition from scrambling onto rocks. Nearby terraces offer another way to watch the evening.

Choose this for: A sunset walk followed by a town dinner. Keep the promenade plan separate from a restaurant reservation: check the terrace’s opening and booking terms if a particular table or music programme matters.

Read the official Ses Variades guide

Sant Josep · Islet horizon

Platges de Comte / Cala Conta

This group of small coves looks toward the western islets. Sant Josep’s tourism guide identifies the islands of des Bosc, sa Conillera and s’Espartar as part of its sunset landscape. Choose it when the coves and sea view are the destination, rather than a stop squeezed between two towns.

Check first: Look up current access and your return. The municipal beach information describes summer-season transport and services; don’t assume those operate on a winter visit. Stay on marked access routes and respect barriers instead of following a photo location onto exposed edges.

Sant Joan · Northern cove

Benirràs

Balearic tourism describes a coarse-sand cove beneath the hills, with fishermen’s huts and the islet of Es Cap Bernat offshore. The shore and the rock’s silhouette give this a different frame from the town promenade or Comte’s wider island horizon.

Choose this for: A dedicated northern-coast outing. Check access arrangements and the conditions on your date, and organise the return before arriving. Choose it for the scenery; an old video of a performance is not a current event listing.

Plan the return as carefully as the view

  1. Use your date, not a copied time. Check the local sunset time and forecast shortly before going. The sun’s position changes through the year; a summer photograph does not guarantee the same alignment with an islet in winter.
  2. Arrive with daylight to spare. Find the designated access and your return route while you can see it. Wear suitable footwear, carry a light, keep away from wave-washed rocks and exposed edges, and choose a sheltered alternative if conditions are poor.
  3. Keep the onward plan realistic. Confirm the last usable transport option or a return pickup for the actual place and date. Don’t rely on a summer bus screenshot, an assumed taxi queue or a restaurant being open after dusk.

A sunset view is not a weather guarantee

Cloud, wind and the chosen position can change the experience. If the forecast or access is unsuitable, change the plan rather than moving beyond barriers for a clearer photograph. Leave the coast as you found it.
