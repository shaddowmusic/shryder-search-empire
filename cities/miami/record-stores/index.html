Three different reasons to visit

Little Haiti · Order ahead

Sweat Records

Sweat’s FAQ makes a useful distinction: its web shop lists new official releases; used records are sold in-store or through its linked Discogs page. A search of the web shop alone won’t tell you what used copies you might find.

For a specific new release: Select local pickup at checkout, then wait for confirmation that the order is ready. Sweat says processing isn’t necessarily immediate. For a used pressing, start with the separate used-record channel or ask the store.

5505 NE 2nd Avenue · Little Haiti

Read Sweat’s pickup and used-record FAQ

NE 79th Street · Compare new and used

Technique Records

Technique’s own site separates new releases, used/rare records and preorders. Use those sections to focus the search, then confirm the exact copy with the shop if you’re making a trip for one title.

For a used find: Bring the artist, label or catalogue number. Ask about record and sleeve condition separately, and whether you may inspect or hear the copy. A preorder listing is not a promise of a record available to take home today.

880 NE 79th Street · Miami

Explore Technique’s catalogue and current contact details

Wynwood · Selling a collection

Lucky Records Miami

If you’re selling rather than shopping, Lucky publishes a collection-buying process: make contact with a description first, then arrange an evaluation. Its stated interests include jazz, soul, Latin, salsa and hip-hop, among other styles. That buying list is not a current shop-stock list.

Before carrying boxes in: Send an outline of formats, artists, quantity and condition, and ask what the shop wants to see. The operator asks for playable records with sleeves; acceptance and any offer depend on its evaluation.

143 NW 23rd Street · Wynwood

Read Lucky’s collection inquiry details

Turn a wish list into a workable stop

  1. Choose one anchor. A confirmed pickup at Sweat, a used-copy search at Technique or a collection evaluation at Lucky gives the trip a purpose. Add a second store only after checking its opening and your route.
  2. Check the edition before paying. Match the format, label, catalogue number and any pressing details that matter. Ask about a return policy before opening a sealed record.
  3. Keep shopping separate from the show. If you’re going out afterward, decide where to leave purchases. Don’t make a bag of LPs part of a concert plan without checking the venue’s bag rules.

Don’t let a web listing stand in for confirmation

New, used, preorder and local pickup can follow different processes, even at the same shop. Save the actual item page and wait for the store’s confirmation before making a dedicated journey.
