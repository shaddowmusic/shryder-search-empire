Three ways to hear live music in Miami

Little Havana · Dance to a live band

Ball & Chain

On Calle Ocho at 1513 SW 8th Street, the venue lists salsa performances alongside events with other formats. If you want to dance to live musicians, choose a listing that names a band; a class or DJ night is a different plan.

Check first: See if your event is ticketed and check the age and ID rules for the time you’ll arrive.

Read the Calle Ocho lineup

Northeast Second Avenue · Walk-in set

Lagniappe

The venue at 3425 NE 2nd Avenue lists live bands and says it doesn’t take reservations. Start with the music schedule: the band’s description tells you more than assuming it will be a straight-ahead jazz night.

Check first: If your group needs to sit together, ask about seating before making this the only plan. No-reservation entry doesn’t guarantee a table.

North Beach · Date-specific concert

Miami Beach Bandshell

Use the concert calendar when there’s an artist you want to hear. The Bandshell is a canopy-covered open-air venue at 7275 Collins Avenue in North Beach, in Miami Beach rather than on the mainland.

Check first: Seating depends on the show. Read your event listing and the venue’s bag policy, and check for weather updates before crossing to the beach.

Find a concert · Check seating, bags and weather

Make the show the anchor

  1. Verify the date in Miami local time. Read the event page, not just a search result labeled “tonight.” Save the act, address, doors and showtime.
  2. Match the entry to the plan. A table booking, a walk-in set and a concert ticket are not interchangeable. Confirm what’s included, especially if you need a seat or step-free access.
  3. Keep dinner and the return close to the show. If the Bandshell is the anchor, make North Beach the plan instead of squeezing in a second mainland show. Check your return from the concert address, not just the label “Miami.”

Make your backup a real option

Look up a second set that works on your date, near the first. If your group can’t get a table or a concert changes, you’ll know where you’re going and what’s playing.
