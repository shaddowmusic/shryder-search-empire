Match the quiet

Which spa format fits your plan?

01

Oriental Spa: heritage house and a formal arrival rhythm

Mandarin Oriental places its spa in a restored teakwood house and publishes a broad treatment approach spanning Thai, Chinese and contemporary techniques. The useful planning signal is procedural: arrive at least 20 minutes early, expect late arrival to shorten the treatment, and check in without active mobile devices.

The operator lists 16 as the general minimum age for Spa and Fitness & Wellness Centre access and says heat experiences may be used after a treatment. Children’s treatment terms are separate, with parental-consent and same-room requirements. Confirm the current treatment, age route and access before booking.

02

Auriga: garden suites and water-therapy facilities

Capella’s Auriga page lists seven treatment suites, including two couples’ suites, plus a vitality pool, water therapy, spa garden and relaxation lounge. It also distinguishes a female steam room and male sauna, so a generic “full facilities” assumption would be misleading.

Choose this format when suite layout and water-based facilities matter. Use the operator’s online booking or contact route to ask what is included with the specific treatment, whether a couples’ suite is available, and which facilities apply to each guest.

Check Auriga Wellness

03

Four Seasons: an explicit day-spa product

The Urban Wellness Centre publishes a current day-pass product that combines one listed treatment with locker rooms, steam and vitality areas, lap pool, fitness centre and changing facilities. That offer has its own time windows, minimum age and availability language; it should not be generalized to every appointment.

This is the clearest format for someone specifically seeking a multi-hour facility day. Reconfirm the active offer, treatment choice, access window, taxes and cancellation policy. A standard treatment booking may carry different inclusions.

Check the Urban Wellness Centre

04

Peninsula: private-suite details and a longer arrival buffer

The Peninsula describes a three-story Thai colonial-style spa building by the river. Private suites may include steam showers and an en-suite whirlpool; the broader spa description also names sauna, steam and plunge pools. Do not assume every room or booking includes every feature.

The operator recommends arriving 30 minutes early and states a minimum age of 16 for access and treatments. Choose this format when suite design and pre-treatment facility time matter, then ask which room type and shared areas accompany your selected appointment.

Before booking

Protect the appointment from avoidable friction

  1. Choose the outcome, not a promise. Decide whether you want Thai massage, oil work, a facial, couples’ space or facility time. This guide makes no medical or therapeutic-outcome claim.
  2. Confirm guest access. Ask whether hotel residency is required, which facilities are included, and whether access begins before or continues after the treatment.
  3. Declare relevant needs. Share pregnancy, injury, allergies, mobility needs or other concerns directly with the spa and seek appropriate professional advice when necessary.
  4. Read the clock. Keep the published arrival buffer and cancellation deadline. Late arrival can reduce treatment time, while a missed cancellation window can trigger charges.
  5. Plan the river connection. Save the exact hotel entrance and allow for road or boat transfers. Do not schedule a separate reservation immediately after the appointment.

Booking questions

Facilities, age rules and quiet

Can I book a hotel spa without staying overnight?

Several official pages provide treatment reservation routes or a day-spa product, but access can vary by appointment. Ask the spa directly whether non-resident booking is available for your date and what it includes.

Does a treatment include the pool, sauna or steam room?

Only when the selected product or spa confirms it. Four Seasons publishes specific day-pass inclusions, while other pages describe facilities without establishing the same entitlement for every treatment.
