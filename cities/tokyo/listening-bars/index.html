01

Bar Martha: let the record lead

Bar Martha’s official notes define the room around records and drinks. They ask visitors not to talk loudly or make noise that interferes with the music, and they prohibit photography inside—including drinks, records and the sound system.

  • Choose it only if the group wants the music to outrank conversation.
  • Put the phone away before entering; no-photo is a published house rule.
  • Do not treat the room as content capture or a casual group bar.

02

Ginza Music Bar: hear across genres

Ginza Music Bar describes a collection of more than 3,000 analog records spanning rock, jazz, soul, disco, new wave, indie, hip-hop, techno, minimal and film music. Its published system includes Garrard, Tannoy and McIntosh components.

  • Choose it when broad selection and a polished salon setting matter.
  • Use the venue’s official contact form or phone for reservation requests.
  • Check the daily-information channel for the date-specific program.

THE MUSIC BAR -CAVE SHIBUYA-: add dinner to the system

The venue’s official site positions high-end audio beside redesigned cocktails and bistro-style organic food. This solves a different intent from a silent listening room: music remains central, but the night can include a complete meal and a reservation.

  • Choose it when sound, cocktails and food need to share one address.
  • Use the official reservation route rather than assuming walk-in space.
  • Check current menus and service details; offerings can change.

A10 Ebisu: choose a modern record-bar night

A10 calls itself a modern record bar, with high-grade audio, selectors programming R&B, soul and jazz across eras, and original cocktails developed under mixology direction. Choose it for a more social adult-night format rather than near-silent listening.

  • Use the genre lane—not a “best bar” label—to decide if it fits.

Before the first record

Match your behavior to the room

  1. Choose attention level. A focused listening room and a social record bar promise different nights.
  2. Check the official page that day. Hours, events, reservation conditions, charges and menus can change.
  3. Read the house rules. Conversation, phones, photography and smoking policies are venue-specific.
  4. Plan one neighborhood. Ebisu, Ginza and Shibuya are separate commitments; do not reduce the evening to a checklist.
  5. Respect the selector. Requests may not suit every room. Listen to the sequence already being built.

The central rule

Do not make the sound compete

If the room asks for quiet, the music is the event. If conversation is welcome, keep it proportionate to the space and the people listening around you.

Common questions

Format, etiquette and choice

What is a Tokyo listening bar?

It is a room where recorded music and the sound system are central. Some spaces expect focused quiet; others combine hi-fi playback with conversation, food, cocktails or selectors.

Which listening bar should I choose?

Choose Bar Martha for strict music-first etiquette, Ginza Music Bar for broad vinyl in a salon setting, CAVE SHIBUYA for high-end sound with bistro food, or A10 for a modern R&B, soul and jazz record-bar format.

Can I take photos?

Never assume. Bar Martha explicitly prohibits photography and asks guests not to talk loudly. Check each venue’s official rules and follow staff guidance inside.
