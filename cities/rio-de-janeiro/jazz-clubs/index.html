Avenida Atlântica · Copacabana

Blue Note Rio: choose the seat and the session

Start here if you want to pick your seat before you arrive. Blue Note Rio publishes a marked seating map and serves food and drinks during shows. Its calendar also spans Brazilian popular music (MPB), bossa nova, blues and rock, so filter by performer and repertoire. If two acts are listed on the same date, choose the session you want at checkout and verify what the ticket covers.

Rua Duvivier 37 · Copacabana

Beco das Garrafas: check which room has the set

The venue traces part of samba-jazz’s history to this address. Its current listings distinguish Little Club from Bottle’s Bar. Read the title and performer notes for each room: a trio may be playing in one while the other has a vocal show. If you’re there for Brazilian instrumental music, an archived Alafiá listing is an example of how the venue bills jazz mixed with Brazilian music, not a date to book.

Match the room to the date · Confirm the address and contact

Read the bill before choosing the room

  1. Start with what you want to hear. Look for instrumental or samba-jazz when you want improvisation over Brazilian rhythms. If the headline says tributo, read who it honors and who’s performing; the tribute could be to an artist outside jazz.
  2. Save the time and room together. At Beco, save the act and room name, not just the street address. At Blue Note, match the session and seat. Confirm whether the listed time is doors or showtime, especially if you’re fitting dinner around the set.
  3. Confirm what the booking includes. If it isn’t clear whether there’s assigned seating or whether a separate reservation covers admission, ask the venue. Check step-free access directly if you need it.

If your first choice is full

Use the official calendars to find a second bill you would actually want to hear. Confirm availability before changing venues; two rooms sharing an address doesn't mean one booking gets you into both.
